# NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)

## Précis

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0), released January 26, 2023, is the United States government's primary voluntary guidance document for managing risks associated with AI systems across their full lifecycle. Developed through a consensus-based, publicly transparent process including multiple RFI rounds and workshops, the framework is structured around four core functions — **Govern**, **Map**, **Measure**, **Manage** — intended to apply across AI types, use cases, and sectors. It is explicitly voluntary; unlike its cybersecurity counterpart, it imposes no compliance requirements. A companion Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1, July 2024) addresses unique risks from generative AI, and a concept note for a critical infrastructure profile was released in April 2026.

The RMF is important for AI governance less as a regulatory instrument than as a standard-setting and coordination mechanism. It provides a shared vocabulary (trustworthiness, AI actor, AI lifecycle) that has been incorporated by reference into EO 14110, the EU-US Trade and Technology Council dialogues, and industry safety frameworks. Its "trustworthiness" framing — emphasizing validity, reliability, safety, security, explainability, privacy, fairness, and accountability as co-equal attributes — represents a broader conception of AI risk than the catastrophic-harm focus of frontier safety frameworks. This breadth makes it more applicable to deployed AI in regulated industries but less action-forcing on questions of existential risk. The RMF does not address compute thresholds, deployment moratoriums, or the specific frontier AI concerns that animate the Bletchley/Seoul/RSP cluster of documents.

**Note:** The raw file captures only the NIST landing page; the full AI RMF 1.0 document (NIST.AI.100-1) is available at https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf. This source page reflects the landing page content only.

## Key terms introduced

- Govern / Map / Measure / Manage — the four core functions of the AI RMF
- Trustworthiness — operationalized as a composite of validity, reliability, safety, security, explainability, privacy, fairness, and accountability
- AI actor — any entity that plays an active role in the AI lifecycle, from design through decommission
