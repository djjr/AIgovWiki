## Précis

The third edition of the Foundation Model Transparency Index (Bommasani et al., Stanford CRFM, 2025) assesses 13 major AI companies across 100 transparency indicators spanning upstream (training data, compute), model (capabilities, evaluations, limitations), and downstream (deployment, usage policies, impact) transparency. The headline finding is a decline: average transparency fell from 58 (2nd edition) to 40 (3rd edition), driven by new entrants with low scores and by some incumbents becoming less transparent over time. IBM scores highest at 95; xAI and Midjourney score lowest at 14. Training data transparency and compute disclosure remain the most systematically deficient areas — no company fully discloses training data composition, and no company discloses train-test overlap, which has direct implications for benchmark validity. Frontier Model Forum members cluster in the middle of the distribution rather than leading.

The FMTI is important for this wiki as a longitudinal empirical baseline for governance claims about voluntary transparency norms. The 2025 edition's declining average is evidence against the hypothesis that competitive or reputational pressures drive transparency improvements over time — a finding that bears on the regulatory markets argument (which assumes that market mechanisms create incentives for demonstrable responsible behavior). The absence of train-test overlap disclosure also connects directly to [[wiki/findings/benchmark-quality-gaps-undermine-governance|Benchmark Quality Gaps]]: governance frameworks that rely on benchmark scores cannot assess whether those scores reflect genuine capability or data contamination.
