## Précis

Ohio HB 628 (introduced December 11, 2025, 136th General Assembly, Rep. T. Mathews) is an IVO licensing framework closely tracking [[wiki/sources/virginia-sb384-2026|Virginia SB 384]] and the [[wiki/definitions/independent-verification-organization|IVO]] pilot in [[wiki/sources/connecticut-sb5-2026|CT SB 5 §33]] in its overall architecture — voluntary participation, [[wiki/definitions/rebuttable-presumption|rebuttable presumption of reasonable care]], advisory council, 10-year document retention, one-year post-employment restriction for council members — but differs from both in several structurally significant ways. The Ohio Attorney General (rather than a technology-specific agency like Virginia's VITA) serves as licensor and houses the AI Safety Advisory Council jointly with the State Auditor. Licensing criteria are outcome-oriented: an IVO plan must specify measurable risk metrics, baselines, targets, and data sources — reflecting an explicit [[wiki/definitions/level-1-level-2-outcomes|Level 1 / Level 2 outcomes]] architecture. The bill covers both AI "models" and AI "applications" (software programs using AI models), introduces "security vendor" as a defined term for third-party red-teaming entities, and builds whistleblower protection requirements into the ongoing-monitoring elements an IVO plan must address.

The most notable structural departure from Virginia is a mandatory revocation trigger: the AG *must* revoke an IVO license if a verified AI model or application causes a material harm of the type the IVO was licensed to prevent. This is a strict ex-post accountability mechanism absent in the Virginia version, which grounds revocation primarily in procedural failures and conflicts of interest. As introduced; no committee action or passage recorded.

## Tensions / contradictions with existing wiki

Ohio's mandatory harm-triggered revocation is more aggressive than Virginia's framework and raises questions the Hadfield regulatory markets literature does not fully resolve: if IVO license revocation voids all verified certificates (the "race-to-bottom countermeasure" documented in [[wiki/findings/race-to-bottom-countermeasure-license-revocation|the Hadfield race-to-bottom finding]]), then harm-triggered revocation creates systemic disruption proportional to the IVO's market penetration. Ohio's draft does not address this cascade risk.
