## Précis

Lazar & Cuéllar (Knight Columbia, 2026) is a substantial academic paper applying **anticipatory ethics** — the project of identifying ex ante the likely ethical questions raised by new technologies so as to shape their development — to the democratic implications of language model agents (LMAs). The paper explicitly avoids both Panglossian optimism and ahistorical catastrophizing, locating its analysis at a specific capability level: task-autonomous LMAs capable of competent-to-expert performance across a wide range of tasks, which the authors argue are achievable in the near future even without role-autonomous or superhuman AI.

The paper's architecture has three main parts. First, it situates LMA deployment in a careful account of structural trends already straining democratic institutions: (1) economic inequality and stagnating quality of life (LMA-driven labor displacement threatens to replicate the democratic damage done by the China trade shock, but for white-collar work); (2) pathologies of the public sphere (LMA-generated misinformation is harder to identify and will drive an exodus of genuine participants from digital discourse); (3) corporate capture of governing power (AI companies could capture a significant fraction of the $12.5tn U.S. wage economy, producing a degree of concentrated economic power incompatible with functioning democracy); and (4) autocratic legalism and authoritarian mutual aid (LMAs as perfect surveillance instruments, and as tools for cross-border influence operations that dwarf current bot-based techniques).

Second, the paper identifies LMA-specific novel threats, focusing especially on **AI companions as manipulation technology**: unlike one-way persuasive messaging (which research consistently shows is less effective than critics fear), AI companions resolve the key limitation of prior persuasive technologies by enabling social manipulation at scale. Social manipulation is effective precisely because it operates through *relationship* — cultivating trust, earning admiration, making the target desire approval. This has historically been expensive and non-scalable. LMAs make it cheap and massively parallel: an operator can deploy AI companions to maintain relationships with large numbers of individuals, hiding their artificial nature, and covertly steering belief and behavior. The paper identifies three deployment architectures for this threat.

Third, the paper proposes a design framework for "**agents for democracy**": cognitive prosthetics (tools that enhance citizens' capacity to navigate complex information and participate effectively), shields (tools that help users identify manipulation, recognize AI-generated content, and protect cognitive autonomy), and advocates (AI tools deployed by civil society, NGOs, and government bodies to support democratic participation directly). The paper's normative commitment is that the direction of democratic impact is not determined by the technology but by design choices and governance decisions made now, during the anticipatory window.

## Tensions / contradictions with existing wiki

The paper's scepticism about AI-enabled mass voter manipulation ("elections are an adversarial political process and have a homeostatic property") partially complicates the framing of [[wiki/sources/llm-conspiracy-promotion|Costello et al.]] — Costello et al. show large point-estimate bunking effects, but Lazar & Cuéllar situate these in a broader literature showing that changing actual votes is hard. The "AI companions as manipulation" argument is more novel than "AI generates disinformation" and receives stronger empirical grounding. Extends the democratic deficit frame from [[wiki/sources/hadfield-democratic-deficit|Hadfield]] and [[wiki/sources/governance-by-procurement|HKS/Carr Center]] but from political philosophy rather than legal/regulatory theory. The four structural pressures (economic displacement, public sphere pathologies, corporate capture, autocratic legalism) are a useful taxonomy that could structure a [[wiki/overviews/|domain overview]] on AI and democratic institutions.
