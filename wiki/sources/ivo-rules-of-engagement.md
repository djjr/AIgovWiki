# IVO Rules of Engagement — Cross-Industry Interaction Patterns

## Précis

A companion table to the regulatory table, focused on *how* third-party verifiers interact with (a) the entity they evaluate and (b) the government authority that oversees them. Covers the same 12 industry/analog pairs as the regulatory table.

Five design questions that the table is organized around: (1) Who initiates the relationship — regulator mandate, market choice, or both? (2) Who pays — regulatee, government, or a levy? This shapes independence. (3) What must be reported upstream to government vs. kept confidential? (4) What access rights does the third party have — scheduled, unannounced, embedded? (5) What enforcement power does the third party hold directly vs. referring to government?

Key patterns surfaced: PCAOB-registered audit firms have no duty to proactively report individual client failures to PCAOB (only discovered via periodic inspections), while nuclear ANIs must report quality-related deficiencies to the NRC. The Joint Commission must report "immediate jeopardy" situations to CMS within 1 business day. FAA DERs must report deviations and safety findings to the FAA. The variation in mandatory upstream reporting is one of the most consequential IVO design variables for AI — what must an AI IVO report to the licensing authority, and on what timeline?
