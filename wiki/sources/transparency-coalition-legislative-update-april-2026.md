# Transparency Coalition: AI Legislative Update, April 24, 2026

## Précis

The Transparency Coalition's weekly AI Legislative Update for April 24, 2026 is the most comprehensive available snapshot of US state AI legislative activity at the peak of the 2026 session cycle. It covers approximately 25 states with active AI-related bills, providing bill numbers, sponsors, vote tallies, and procedural status for hundreds of measures. The document is a primary empirical source on the breadth, thematic clustering, and geographic distribution of the 2026 state legislative wave.

The dominant legislative themes across states are: (1) **chatbot/companion AI safety** (minor-protective provisions, disclosure requirements, mental health prohibitions) — enacted or in advanced stages in Oregon, Nebraska, Idaho, Tennessee, Georgia, Washington, and pending in Arizona, California, Colorado, Iowa, Michigan, Minnesota, and others; (2) **healthcare AI regulation** — prohibitions on AI-only insurance decisions enacted or advancing in Alabama, Georgia, Hawaii, New Hampshire, Utah, Washington; (3) **deepfake and synthetic media** — expanding to cover AI-generated CSAM, political campaign materials, intimate imagery, and general content disclosure; (4) **employment AI** — disclosure and anti-discrimination provisions advancing in California, Rhode Island, Vermont; (5) **AI personhood prohibition** — Tennessee SB 837 (enacted, 93-2), Missouri and Ohio bills in play, defining AI as non-sentient and non-person.

Several notable enacted or near-enacted bills: Oregon SB 1546 (chatbot safety, signed March 31); Tennessee SB 1580 (therapy chatbot ban, signed April 1); Tennessee SB 837 (AI personhood prohibition, sent to governor April 15); Nebraska LB 1185 (chatbot safety, signed April 17); Utah passed 9 AI bills in a 7-week session (all signed by governor). Florida's DeSantis AI Bill of Rights (SB 482) died when the House declined to act — the most significant failure of a comprehensive bill in the 2026 cycle.

IVO-specific developments: Minnesota HF 4544 / SF 4636 would establish a state IVO license (with Commerce Committee); Ohio HB 628 similarly proposes an IVO license framework (with Technology & Innovation Committee). Virginia HB 797 is described as signed (April 13 was the governor's deadline) with four Virginia bills total sent to Gov. Spanberger. California SB 813 (AI Standards and Safety Commission) passed the Senate 31-7 on January 27 and has been sent to the Assembly — a potential state-level analogue to a centralized AI safety authority.

## Tensions / contradictions with existing wiki

- The Transparency Coalition tracker confirms Virginia HB 797 (the IVO licensing bill) is the companion to SB 384 — both bills were sent to Gov. Spanberger and signed April 13. This is consistent with the source page for Virginia SB 384.
- Florida's SB 482 failure is significant: Gov. DeSantis's own AI Bill of Rights died because the House did not act, suggesting even executive-backed comprehensive AI legislation remains fragile in states with divided legislative appetite. This contrasts with Connecticut's 131-17 House vote and Virginia's 84-14 / 40-0 margins.
- California's legislative pipeline is by far the most voluminous: dozens of bills in committee simultaneously. Whether California produces enacted legislation in 2026 on the order of Virginia or Connecticut, or whether the volume produces gridlock, is the single most consequential open question in state AI governance at this writing.
- Minnesota's IVO bill (HF 4544 / SF 4636) in committee alongside the broader Minnesota AI stack suggests the IVO model is spreading legislatively even before Virginia's framework is operational.
