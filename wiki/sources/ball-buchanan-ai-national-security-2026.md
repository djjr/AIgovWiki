# Ball & Buchanan: A.I. Is a National Security Risk (NYT, May 2026)

## Précis

Published May 4, 2026 in the New York Times Opinion section. Co-authored by Dean Ball (senior fellow, Foundation for American Innovation; served as senior policy adviser for AI and emerging technology at the White House OSTP in 2025 — i.e., Trump administration) and Ben Buchanan (assistant professor, Johns Hopkins SAIS; White House special adviser for AI under Biden). The co-authorship across administrations is the political signal: both explicitly acknowledge their different approaches while claiming enough common ground to issue a joint call to action. Note: Buchanan is disclosed as an outside adviser to Anthropic.

The op-ed's core claim is that AI has become both enormously powerful and an immediate national security risk, and that the U.S. government lacks urgency and a strategic plan. The argument has three components:

**Frontier capability escalation.** The piece offers the most explicit public documentation yet of frontier models achieving dangerous-capability-evaluation thresholds. Anthropic's "Claude Mythos Preview" model is described as having found "thousands of critical vulnerabilities in the basic applications that make computers and the internet work — some of these vulnerabilities were decades old, lurking in code long thought to be clean." OpenAI's GPT-5.4 "now consistently outperforms Ph.D.-level virologists at troubleshooting lab experiments in their areas of focus." Mythos "matches top human experts in some capabilities essential to create and deploy bioweapons." These are operational statements, not theoretical — they describe current deployed systems.

**Compute governance recommendations.** To preserve the U.S. compute advantage (claimed as ~100x China's compute capacity for AI training, with essentially every AI system trained on U.S. chips): (1) tighten export restrictions on chips at or above H200 level; (2) require licenses before foreign buyers of large chip quantities can receive hardware; (3) restrict Chinese access to restricted chips installed in facilities outside China that can be accessed remotely (currently unrestricted); (4) tighten controls on chip manufacturing equipment including foreign-produced equipment with U.S. technology. The authors support international cooperation on catastrophic risks (AI-enabled bioweapons) while maintaining competitive controls — citing nuclear nonproliferation as the precedent.

**Domestic governance recommendations.** Congress should: "mandate audits of AI developers' safety claims and processes, requiring that they be conducted by independent expert bodies overseen by the government." Additionally: age limits and parental control systems for children's safety; additional resources and experts for the AI Safety Institute (renamed in the Trump administration). The AI Safety Institute "languished without a director for months." The bipartisan framing: "Bipartisanship on A.I. is a strategic necessity."

The mandatory audit call is the most significant domestic governance recommendation for this wiki: it is a bipartisan call for what is structurally equivalent to a federal IVO mandate — independent expert bodies, government-overseen, auditing developer safety claims. Neither author uses the IVO terminology, but the institutional design they describe is the IVO model.

## Key terms introduced

None new to wiki. "Claude Mythos Preview" is a named model; "GPT-5.4" is named. These are frontier capability evidence data points rather than conceptual terms.

## Tensions / contradictions with existing wiki

- The claim that Mythos matches experts in "some capabilities essential to create and deploy bioweapons" is the first public statement by someone with apparent inside knowledge (Buchanan is an Anthropic adviser) that an Anthropic model has reached a dangerous capability evaluation threshold for CBRN harm. This directly advances the [[wiki/findings/converging-risk-domains-bio-cyber-autonomy|biosecurity/cybersecurity/autonomy convergence]] finding with current empirical data.
- The mandatory audit call creates tension with the voluntary participation design documented in [[wiki/findings/voluntary-ivo-design-tradeoff|Voluntary IVO Participation: Design Tradeoff]]. If Congress mandates audits, the adverse selection problem dissolves — but the political viability of a federal mandate, given the Trump administration's deregulatory posture, is unclear. The fact that a Trump-era official (Ball) is co-signing this call is significant.
- The compute advantage claim (~100x China) partially contradicts the framing in [[wiki/findings/china-export-controls-as-ai-safety-risk|China Frames Export Controls as an AI Safety Risk]] — where Chinese officials frame the export controls as a threat to AI safety. Ball and Buchanan's piece argues the controls are necessary for American security; China's Framework 2.0 argues they threaten global AI safety. Same fact (chip access asymmetry), opposite normative framings.
- The AI Safety Institute characterization — "languished without a director for months and urgently needs additional resources" — is consistent with the deregulatory posture documented in [[wiki/findings/us-executive-ai-governance-reversal-2025|U.S. Executive AI Governance Reversal (January 2025)]] but notable because both a Trump and a Biden official are jointly calling for its strengthening.
