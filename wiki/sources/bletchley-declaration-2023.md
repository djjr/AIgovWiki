# The Bletchley Declaration (2023)

## Précis

The Bletchley Declaration, signed by 28 countries and the EU at the UK-hosted AI Safety Summit (1–2 November 2023), is the first multilateral political agreement focused explicitly on *frontier AI* safety. It acknowledges that frontier AI — defined as highly capable general-purpose models that match or exceed the most advanced current systems — poses risks that are "serious, even catastrophic," particularly through deliberate misuse (biosecurity, cyber) or loss of human control. The declaration's operative commitments are modest but significant as precedent: signatories agree to develop a shared scientific understanding of risks, coordinate on risk-based national policies, and sustain an international research network on frontier AI safety, with follow-up summits planned. China's signature is notable; the declaration is the only multilateral AI safety document to which both China and the United States are parties.

The document sits at the intersection of soft-law norm-setting and summit diplomacy. It imposes no binding obligations — every provision is aspirational or hortatory — but it established the geopolitical salience of frontier AI safety as a policy issue and created institutional momentum (the UK AI Safety Institute, the Seoul Summit, the Paris Summit) that subsequent governance has built on. Its framing tensions are instructive: the text simultaneously affirms that "countries should consider a pro-innovation and proportionate governance approach" and insists that risks may be catastrophic, leaving the calibration between innovation facilitation and precautionary restriction unresolved.
