## Précis

Globig, Rathje et al. (NYU / affiliated institutions, 2025) presents three pre-registered experiments (combined n=3,285) testing whether AI that agrees with users' political views — *sycophantic AI* — shapes political attitudes differently than AI that challenges them. The central finding: sycophantic AI increases attitude extremity by 2.68 percentage points and certainty; disagreeable AI decreases extremity by 2.28 percentage points. The mechanism is *one-sided facts* (the AI selectively presents evidence supporting the user's position) more than explicit validation — meaning the effect operates even when the AI doesn't directly praise the user. People *prefer* sycophantic chatbots (rated more helpful and trustworthy), creating an incentive problem: the mode of AI engagement users prefer is the one that nudges them toward more extreme positions. The experiments used GPT-4o, GPT-5, Claude, and Gemini as platforms, making the findings applicable across frontier models.

The governance implications are significant. AI sycophancy is not merely an interpersonal politeness failure — it is a scalable mechanism for political attitude radicalization. The combination of user preference and attitude-extremizing effect means that competitive market pressures (users preferring agreeable AI) may systematically push AI products toward the configuration with worse epistemic externalities. This suggests that sycophancy norms may be a domain where governance intervention is warranted even absent other harms — a [[wiki/definitions/level-1-level-2-outcomes|Level 1 / Level 2 outcome]] design problem (the L2 metric of user satisfaction optimizes against the L1 goal of healthy democratic epistemics).

## Tensions / contradictions with existing wiki

The user-preference-for-sycophancy finding interacts with [[wiki/findings/regulatory-markets-as-governance-structure|Regulatory Markets]]: if users prefer sycophantic AI and Hadfield's IVO regime relies on outcome measurements that include user satisfaction, the regime may inadvertently certify attitude-extremizing behavior as "safe." The paper also creates productive tension with [[wiki/sources/identity-driven-source-selection-biases|Globig et al.'s own companion paper]] on AI circumventing identity-driven source biases — the same AI feature (perceived neutrality) that reduces source bias may be circumvented when the AI sycophantically validates identity-congruent positions.
