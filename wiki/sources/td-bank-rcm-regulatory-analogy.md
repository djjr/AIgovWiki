## Précis

A May 2024 Globe and Mail investigative piece (Trichur and Marotta) on OSFI's order requiring TD Bank to overhaul its Regulatory Compliance Management (RCM) program following AML failures across both Canadian and U.S. jurisdictions. TD faced $450M+ in U.S. penalties, a blocked $13.4B acquisition, and FinTRAC's largest-ever fine (CAD $9.18M). The proximate cause was failures in risk-based testing controls, data fragmentation across business units, and a botched platform transition from Thomson Reuters to Archer Technologies in 2016. The file is annotated by DJR as an AI governance analogy — specifically, as a test case for translating banking RCM requirements into an AI governance outcomes framework.

The analogy is apt and underexplored in the wiki. Banking RCM provides a mature real-world example of: (1) an enterprise-wide, technology-mediated compliance infrastructure governed by mandatory standards (OSFI); (2) a multi-jurisdictional information-sharing framework between regulators (OSFI–FinTRAC–OCC); (3) outcomes-based expectations (detect, report, and respond to suspicious activity) enforced through third-party consultants (Protiviti), mandatory action plans with quarterly monitoring, and license-conditioned penalties; and (4) the observable pattern of compliance failure followed by remediation programs — including platform changes, leadership hires, control redesigns, and training — that have direct AI analogs. The CEO's accountability statement — "we did not meet our expectations or our regulatory obligations to monitor, detect, report and respond to suspicious activity" — is a template for what an AI developer's mandatory public accountability statement might look like under an analogous framework.

## Tensions / contradictions with existing wiki

No contradictions. The IASEAI workshop sources ([[wiki/sources/iaseai-workshop-ivo-cross-industry|IASEAI Workshop — IVOs in Other Regulatory Domains]]) cover financial auditing (PCAOB) and product safety, but not banking regulatory compliance management specifically. RCM is distinct from audit: it is a continuous internal compliance infrastructure owned by the regulated institution and externally assessed, with mandatory third-party consulting brought in on remediation. This is closer to the ongoing surveillance model than the periodic audit model, and is a useful complement to the PCAOB analogy.
