# IASEAI Workshop — Outcomes Framework for IVO Regulation

## Précis

Three draft versions (Dan Ryan, IASEAI workshop series) of a cross-industry outcomes framework applying the Level 1 / Level 2 distinction to eight regulatory domains: product safety, financial services, food and agriculture, environment (vehicle emissions and factory emissions), healthcare, infrastructure (bridges), aviation, and maritime. The tabular version (draft 2) is the canonical form; drafts 1 and 3 are narrative predecessors.

The central analytical move is distinguishing what regulation ultimately wants (Level 1: "bridges don't collapse," "AI doesn't assist bioweapon development") from what it actually measures and enforces (Level 2: "inspections every 24 months," "model red-team scores below threshold X"). The framework documents five structural patterns that appear across all industries: (1) from harm prevention to compliance verification; (2) from outcomes to measurable proxies; (3) from absolutes to thresholds; (4) from broad goals to specific processes; (5) from prevention to detection + response. The Level 1/Level 2 gap reveals where regulatory systems rely on process compliance as proxy for outcome achievement, where they define acceptable risk through thresholds rather than prevention, and where verification systems have inherent blind spots.

For AI IVO design specifically, this framework makes the following demand practical: IVOs must be assessed by government against Level 1 outcomes (does certification correlate with actual safety?) not just Level 2 proxies (did the certified company pass the tests?). This is harder to operationalize than in mature regulatory domains because AI Level 1 outcomes (no catastrophic misuse) are rarer, harder to attribute, and potentially irreversible.

## Tensions / contradictions with existing wiki

The Level 2 metrics currently used in [[wiki/definitions/responsible-capability-scaling|Responsible Capability Scaling]] (ASL thresholds, CCL evaluations) are themselves Level 2 proxies for Level 1 outcomes (no catastrophic harm). The framework raises the question of how well-calibrated these proxies are and who verifies the calibration.
