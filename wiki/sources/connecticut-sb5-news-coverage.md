# Connecticut SB 5 (2026) — News Coverage of Passage

## Précis

Two contemporaneous news accounts — Pluribus News and CT Mirror, both May 1-2, 2026 — cover the House passage of Connecticut SB 5 (131-17) and provide the political context missing from the bill text itself. Together they document a three-year legislative arc: Sen. Maroney's 2024 and 2025 comprehensive anti-discrimination bills died under veto threats or House inaction; the 2026 bill narrowed its scope to worker disclosure, frontier whistleblower protection, and AI companion safety, and secured a bipartisan supermajority by absorbing elements of the governor's own requested legislation (regulatory sandbox, youth social media protections).

The most valuable contribution of these articles is the negotiation record: the explanatory-statement requirement for adverse employment decisions was removed at the governor's request; the regulatory sandbox was added to secure executive support; and the bill was explicitly rebranded as the AI Responsibility and Transparency Act on the day of House passage to signal its narrower accountability framing. Industry opposition (CCIA, NetChoice) was present but muted — NetChoice's argument was primarily preemption-based (wait for federal law), not substantive opposition to the bill's individual provisions.

The articles also document the state-federal tension directly: the Trump administration's stated goal of preempting state AI regulations was explicitly noted during floor debate, and Connecticut legislators proceeded anyway. Maroney's framing — that the bill's transparency-and-disclosure focus doesn't "make Connecticut more of a target" — is the strategic logic that distinguished the 2026 approach from the 2024-2025 attempts, which were framed as anti-discrimination law and thus more vulnerable to preemption and business opposition.
