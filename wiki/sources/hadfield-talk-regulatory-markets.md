# Hadfield Talk — Regulatory Markets for AI Governance

## Précis

Gillian Hadfield's structured talk (April 2025, attributed to GKH SGP) presents a three-part argument for why AI requires a new regulatory architecture. First, conventional "command and control" regulation fails for machine learning because its logic assumes human-readable rules — a regulator can audit a process, understand it, and write specific rules. ML breaks this: the training process produces decision logic that is not legible to human auditors or courts. The result is a "governance gap" that grows faster than the policy cycle (typically 3–5 years per regulation). Hadfield names this "Robo-economicus" — AI as a new economic actor whose intentions and logic cannot be read by existing legal frameworks.

Second, the proposed solution is Regulatory Markets: government acts as goal-setter and licensor rather than technical auditor. It defines democratic outcomes ("AI must not assist bioweapon development"; "AI must not exhibit racial bias in credit scoring") and licenses Independent Verification Organizations (IVOs) — private competitive firms whose business model is auditing AI developers. The market incentive mechanism is the Rebuttable Presumption of Reasonable Care: IVO-certified developers are treated by law as having taken reasonable care, providing a shield against tort liability and a prerequisite for insurance. Uncertified developers are uninsurable and legally exposed. The race-to-bottom risk (IVOs issuing "easy" certificates) is addressed by license revocation: if an IVO is found to be rubber-stamping unsafe models, its license is revoked, all its certificates become void, and every company using them loses liability protection. Market participants therefore prefer rigorous certification over cheap certification.

Third, Hadfield argues this scales globally without requiring a "Global AI Government." A shared technical standard for what an IVO does allows the US and EU to set different substantive rules while sharing the same market of technical auditors. This aligns with how maritime classification societies (Lloyd's, ABS) serve multiple flag states under a shared technical standard.

## Tensions / contradictions with existing wiki

The IVO proposal directly addresses the gap noted in [[wiki/definitions/responsible-capability-scaling|Responsible Capability Scaling]]: that safety evaluations under ASL/CCL frameworks are currently assessed primarily by developers themselves. IVOs would replace or supplement developer self-assessment with certified third-party verification. This is also in tension with the [[wiki/definitions/risk-based-approach|Risk-Based Approach (AI regulation)]] of the EU AI Act, which uses prescriptive top-down classification rather than outcome-based market licensing.
