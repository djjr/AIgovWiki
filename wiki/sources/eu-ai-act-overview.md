# EU AI Act Overview (Regulation EU 2024/1689)

## Précis

The EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), which entered into force on August 1, 2024, is the world's first comprehensive legal framework for AI. It adopts a **risk-based approach** organized around four tiers: unacceptable risk (banned outright), high-risk (strict pre-market obligations), transparency risk (disclosure requirements), and minimal/no risk (unregulated). The eight banned practices — including social scoring, real-time biometric identification in public spaces, and exploitation of vulnerabilities — became effective February 2025. High-risk obligations apply from August 2026 and 2027. Rules for **general-purpose AI (GPAI)** models, including systemic-risk models, became applicable August 2025.

The Act's GPAI provisions are architecturally significant. They recognize that the risk-based tier structure, designed for specific use cases, cannot straightforwardly classify foundation models that may be embedded in any application. GPAI providers must meet transparency and copyright obligations; those whose models carry "systemic risks" (operationalized as ≥10²⁵ FLOPs training compute, roughly analogous to the U.S. EO 14110 threshold) face additional evaluation and mitigation requirements. The Commission published a GPAI Code of Practice in July 2025 as a voluntary compliance instrument. The Act represents the most structurally advanced attempt to regulate the AI stack — covering models, systems, providers, and deployers — in a single binding instrument. A Digital Package on Simplification (adopted November 2025) proposes targeted amendments, including streamlining SME obligations and centralizing oversight of GPAI-built systems in the EU AI Office.

For comparative regulatory analysis, the EU AI Act is the primary reference point for what binding, rights-oriented AI regulation looks like — as opposed to the U.S. approach of voluntary standards and executive action, or the UK's sector-led, pro-innovation regulatory philosophy.

## Key terms introduced

- [[wiki/definitions/risk-based-approach|Risk-Based Approach (AI regulation)]] — the organizing principle: regulatory obligations scale with risk level rather than applying uniformly
- [[wiki/definitions/general-purpose-ai|General-Purpose AI (GPAI)]] — AI models that can perform a wide range of tasks and serve as foundations for many systems; subject to distinct obligations under the Act
- [[wiki/definitions/high-risk-ai|High-Risk AI (EU AI Act)]] — AI use cases in sensitive domains (employment, credit, law enforcement, biometrics, education, critical infrastructure) subject to pre-market conformity assessment
- Systemic risk (GPAI) — risk arising from GPAI models at scale; triggered by ≥10²⁵ FLOPs training compute or Commission designation

## Tensions / contradictions with existing wiki

- The Act's compute-based GPAI systemic-risk threshold (10²⁵ FLOPs) is lower than EO 14110's dual-use foundation model threshold (10²⁶ FLOPs) — suggesting the EU applies heavier obligations at a lower capability level.
- The Act does not directly address catastrophic/existential risks from frontier AI in the same terms as Bletchley/Seoul; its primary concern is with discrimination, fundamental rights, and systemic societal harm.
