# Hadfield & Freeman — Cognitive Revolution Podcast (July 2025)

## Précis

A Cognitive Revolution podcast with Gillian Hadfield (Bloomberg Distinguished Professor, Johns Hopkins) and Andrew Freeman (co-founder, Fathom) covering the regulatory markets proposal and its instantiation as California SB813. The podcast largely covers the same ground as the Hadfield talk but adds several important threads.

Freeman's framing: Fathom's research found near-universal agreement that (1) AI governance is needed and (2) neither heavy top-down regulation nor pure laissez-faire will work. This led to interest in a "third way" — market-based governance. He describes the proposal through the Underwriters Laboratories (UL) analogy: UL started as a private inspection body at a world's fair and was organically adopted by industry before eventually being codified into law. The proposal is to design that outcome deliberately and at speed, with public accountability built in from the start.

The podcast's substantive additions beyond the talk: (1) Hadfield and Jack Clark co-authored a 2019 paper proposing this model for AI safety when Clark was policy director at OpenAI — the idea predates the current governance debate by six years. (2) SB813 is described as a "rebuttable presumption" implementation rather than a hard liability shield — certification counts as *evidence* of reasonable care, not an absolute defense. (3) The character.ai lawsuit (survived motion to dismiss) is cited as evidence that AI companies will face tort liability as agentic AI becomes an "actor in the world," making the liability protection mechanism non-hypothetical. (4) The race-to-bottom risk is addressed by government oversight capacity: "the model is only as good as the capacity for your government to have oversight of these private actors." (5) Hadfield explicitly frames regulatory markets as "one tool in the toolbox," not a replacement for all other regulatory mechanisms — catastrophic-risk domains (bioweapons at civilizational scale) may require separate regulatory regimes entirely.

## Tensions / contradictions with existing wiki

The podcast explicitly frames SB813 as California-state legislation, raising the US governance reversal finding ([[wiki/findings/us-governance-reversal-2025|US Governance Reversal 2025]]): the federal vacuum under EO 14179 may increase the salience of state-level proposals like SB813. Hadfield's critique of top-down prescriptive regulation applies directly to both the EU AI Act ([[wiki/definitions/risk-based-approach|Risk-Based Approach (AI regulation)]]) and Biden-era EO 14110.
