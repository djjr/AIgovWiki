## Précis

This Rockefeller Foundation / Bellagio interview with Gillian Hadfield (2023) is a short but rhetorically powerful statement of the democratic deficit problem in AI governance. Hadfield's core claim: AI is already being governed right now, but only inside the companies developing it — which is not democratic. The decisions that matter most (what to build, who gets to build it, what constraints apply) need to be "securely rooted in our democratic institutions and processes." She is explicit that framing the problem as "ethics" and relying on codes of conduct and data scientist training is insufficient; what is needed is *regulation*, and specifically a regulatory infrastructure that gives governments the ability to know who is building powerful models, to require disclosure, and to pull a lever when behavior is unacceptable. Her concrete minimum: national registry offices requiring registration of powerful model development, analogized to car registration as a precondition for investigating safety.

The interview is short but connects several strands of Hadfield's broader research agenda: the argument that AI challenges *both* the targets *and* the instruments of regulation (developed more fully in the CIFAR paper), the case for novel regulatory forms rather than finger-wagging at companies (developed in her regulatory markets work), and the international framing (her Bellagio residency project was titled "Reinventing global governance for AI"). The democratic deficit framing has become more urgent since 2023 — the procurement governance standoffs of 2026 are precisely the type of outcome she was anticipating.

## Tensions / contradictions with existing wiki

Consistent with Hadfield's other work in the wiki but notably more minimalist in its concrete proposals — the registry/disclosure argument is narrower than the full Regulatory Impacts Analysis framework developed in the CIFAR paper, and narrower than the regulatory markets model. The democratic deficit framing is partially addressed by [[wiki/sources/singapore-consensus-ai-safety-research|Singapore Consensus]] institutional architecture but that work focuses on safety research coordination, not democratic accountability of development decisions.
