# IASEAI Workshop — IVOs in Other Regulatory Domains

## Précis

Background document prepared by Dan Ryan (January 2026) for the IASEAI workshop series on AI governance. Surveys regulatory domains in which government-licensed private entities perform inspection, evaluation, and certification functions analogous to proposed AI IVOs: product safety (NRTLs/UL, EU Notified Bodies), financial services (PCAOB, NRSROs), food and agriculture (USDA organic certifiers), environment (vehicle emissions stations, NELAP labs), healthcare (Joint Commission, clinical lab accreditors), transportation (bridge inspectors, FAA DERs), maritime (classification societies), and nuclear (Authorized Nuclear Inspection Agencies).

Two case studies are developed in detail. Vehicle emissions inspection: state-licensed private test stations with real-time database monitoring, covert audit vehicles, and anomaly detection — a model of continuous quantitative oversight that is harder to capture than periodic inspection. PCAOB (financial audit): mandatory registration system with annual inspections of large firms, public inspection reports, and escalating enforcement (remedial plans → fines → suspension → revocation → SEC/DOJ referral). The PCAOB model is notable for applying to an industry where the auditor is paid by the entity being audited — the same structural tension that will face AI IVOs.

The key cross-industry finding: there is no single best model. Mechanisms range from direct government licensing (FAA DERs, state emissions stations) to accreditation-based recognition (NELAP labs, USDA organic certifiers) to formal designation (EU Notified Bodies, maritime Recognized Organizations). The choice of mechanism reflects the specifics of the regulatory problem — particularly the degree of embedded vs. episodic oversight required, and whether the government or the regulatee initiates the relationship.
