# OECD AI Principles (2019, updated 2024)

## Précis

The OECD Recommendation on Artificial Intelligence (initially adopted May 2019, updated May 2024) is the first intergovernmental standard on AI and remains the most widely adhered-to international AI policy instrument, with 47 adherents including all OECD members, the EU, and G20 partners. It consists of five values-based principles — inclusive growth/sustainable development; human rights and democratic values; transparency and explainability; robustness/security/safety; and accountability — accompanied by five policy recommendations for governments. The 2024 update incorporated developments in generative AI and large language models while preserving the original structure.

The principles' governance significance is largely definitional and coordinative: the OECD's definition of an "AI system" (a machine-based system that infers outputs from inputs to influence physical or virtual environments, varying in autonomy and adaptiveness) has been adopted by the EU AI Act, Council of Europe Convention, United States (via EO 14110), and UN frameworks, making it the de facto standard definition in multilateral governance. This definitional consensus matters because scope of regulation depends on what counts as an AI system — ambiguity here creates regulatory arbitrage opportunities. The principles themselves are sufficiently high-level to command near-universal adherence without constraining national implementation choices substantially, which explains both their breadth of adoption and their limited direct enforceability. They function more as a legitimating framework for national and regional regulation than as a governance mechanism in their own right.

The OECD AI Policy Observatory (oecd.ai) — a companion platform — tracks national AI policies and strategies globally, making it a primary data source for comparative AI governance research.

## Key terms introduced

- OECD AI system definition — "a machine-based system that, for explicit or implicit objectives, infers, from the input it receives, how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions that can influence physical or virtual environments"
- AI actor — any entity playing a role in the AI system lifecycle; OECD usage broadly consistent with NIST RMF

## Tensions / contradictions with existing wiki

- The OECD principles' breadth of adoption (47 adherents including China's G20 partners) contrasts with the difficulty of achieving binding commitments on frontier AI safety, suggesting normative consensus at the level of abstract values does not translate to behavioral convergence.
