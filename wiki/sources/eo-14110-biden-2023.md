# Executive Order 14110: Safe, Secure, and Trustworthy AI (Biden, 2023)

## Précis

Executive Order 14110, signed October 30, 2023 and published November 1, 2023 (88 FR 75191), was the most comprehensive federal AI governance action in U.S. history at time of issue. It established eight guiding principles — safety/security, innovation, workers, equity, consumers, privacy, federal capacity, and global leadership — and directed more than fifty specific agency actions with defined timelines. It was revoked by EO 14148 on January 20, 2025, the first day of the Trump administration.

The order's most consequential provisions were in Section 4. It directed NIST to develop red-teaming guidelines and safety evaluation benchmarks; required companies developing *dual-use foundation models* (defined by broad training, ≥tens of billions of parameters, and CBRN-uplift potential) to report to the government on training activities and red-team results; and introduced explicit **compute thresholds** — 10²⁶ floating-point operations for general dual-use models, 10²³ for biology-focused models — as a proxy for triggering regulatory attention. The compute threshold approach is conceptually significant: it attempts to operationalize "frontier AI" via a measurable physical quantity, creating a governance hook that does not depend on evaluating model behavior post-hoc. Sections on biosecurity (screening of synthetic nucleic acid providers), synthetic content watermarking, and critical infrastructure protection established precedents that have persisted in successor policy discussions even after revocation.

EO 14110 should be read as the high-water mark of U.S. executive branch engagement with AI safety in the Biden era and as a benchmark against which subsequent policy retreats can be measured. Its Section 2 principles — particularly equity, workers' rights, and civil rights — reflect a domestic justice framing that distinguishes it from the primarily national-security and competitiveness framing of the Trump successor order.

## Key terms introduced

- [[wiki/definitions/dual-use-foundation-model|Dual-Use Foundation Model]] — defined at 15 U.S.C. 9401(3) as extended in Sec. 3(k): broad training, ≥tens of billions of parameters, CBRN-uplift potential
- [[wiki/definitions/compute-threshold|Compute Threshold (AI governance)]] — 10²⁶ FLOPs as trigger for dual-use foundation model reporting requirements
- [[wiki/definitions/red-teaming|Red-Teaming (AI)]] — "a structured testing effort to find flaws and vulnerabilities in an AI system, often in a controlled environment" (Sec. 3(d))
- Watermarking — embedding difficult-to-remove information in AI-generated content for provenance tracking (Sec. 3(gg))

## Tensions / contradictions with existing wiki

- EO 14110's equity/workers framing (Sec. 2(c)–(d)) is largely absent from the international frontier safety frameworks, which focus on catastrophic risks rather than distributional harms — reflecting different threat models and political coalitions.
