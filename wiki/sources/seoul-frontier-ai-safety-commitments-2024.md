# Frontier AI Safety Commitments, AI Seoul Summit 2024

## Précis

The Seoul Frontier AI Safety Commitments (May 2024) are voluntary undertakings by 16 major AI developers — including Anthropic, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, and Zhipu.ai — organized around three outcomes: effective risk identification and management; internal accountability; and appropriate external transparency. Announced at the AI Seoul Summit and explicitly linked to the Bletchley process, the document advances from political declaration to industry-level behavioral commitment. Signatories agreed to publish safety frameworks focused on severe risks by the Paris Summit, a public accountability mechanism with a defined deadline.

The substantive core lies in commitments I through IV of Outcome 1. Companies must (I) assess risks across the AI lifecycle; (II) define quantitative or qualitative *thresholds* at which risks become intolerable, with input from home governments; (III) articulate mitigation plans; and (IV) commit not to develop or deploy a model if mitigations cannot keep risks below thresholds. This last provision is the closest the voluntary regime has come to an explicit deployment moratorium commitment. Outcome 3 introduces a two-tier transparency model: public disclosure where feasible, but detailed sharing with "trusted actors" including home governments where public disclosure would create risk — an architecturally important distinction that acknowledges the dual-use nature of safety information itself.

The document is significant less for what it requires than for what it inaugurates: an emerging international norm that frontier developers have *special* responsibilities, that risk thresholds should be pre-defined rather than evaluated post-hoc, and that governments have a legitimate role in receiving non-public safety information from developers in their jurisdictions.

## Key terms introduced

- [[wiki/definitions/frontier-ai|Frontier AI]] — "highly capable general-purpose AI models or systems that can perform a wide variety of tasks and match or exceed the capabilities present in the most advanced models"
- Risk thresholds — quantitative or qualitative levels at which risks are deemed intolerable; must be pre-defined and externally verifiable
- Home government — the government of the country where the AI developer is headquartered; key interlocutor for non-public safety disclosures
