# Start Here

A working wiki on **AI governance** — built to support an interdisciplinary
academic program whose faculty come from law, philosophy, economics, the
social sciences, the humanities, and computer science. The wiki collects
what the program's designers and teachers want at hand: vocabularies,
claims, sources, comparative regimes, syllabus material, and the live
arguments between them. It grows whenever a new source lands in `raw/` and
is ingested.

## How to use this wiki

The fastest way to orient yourself is the
[[AI-Governance-LLM-wiki/wiki/overview|Overview]] — the running synthesis
of what the wiki currently knows and is currently thinking, including the
five live tensions that structure most of the field. The
[[AI-Governance-LLM-wiki/wiki/index|Index]] is the catalog: every page,
sortable by type. The graph view in Obsidian is the map; follow the edges.

## What's in here

The wiki organizes material into four domains in conversation, plus a
cross-cutting layer of risk frames and structural levers. As pages
accumulate, the brief sketches below grow into directories of links.

**Technical AI safety.** Alignment, interpretability, evaluations, scalable
oversight, dangerous-capability assessment. The conceptual machinery and
empirical findings on the ML side, kept legible to readers without ML
training.

**Governance and regulatory theory.** What regulation is, when it works,
how institutions form, why they capture or fail. The general intellectual
stock without which AI-specific policy is rootless: administrative law,
risk regulation, soft vs. hard law, multistakeholder governance, and
analogues from nuclear, pharmaceutical, aviation, financial, and
environmental regimes.

**AI-specific policy.** The concrete instruments. The EU AI Act, US
executive actions and state laws, the UK AI Safety Institute and its
peers, China's generative-AI and recommendation rules, and multilateral
processes (Bletchley → Seoul → Paris, OECD, G7 Hiroshima). Comparative work
across these sits here.

**Risk frames.** Existential, misuse (bio, cyber, persuasion), systemic
(labor, market concentration, epistemic ecosystem), fairness and
discrimination, democratic and civic, environmental. The wiki treats these
as overlapping rather than competing.

**Pedagogy.** Curriculum design, learning outcomes, case methods, tabletop
exercises, policy memos, assessment. The reason the wiki exists.

## Conventions

Pages come in five types — *definition*, *explanation*, *finding*,
*source*, and *query* — explained in
[[AI-Governance-LLM-wiki/CLAUDE|CLAUDE.md]]. **Definitions** are short and
exist mainly as hover targets. **Findings** are first-class pages so a
single claim can be cited and contested across the graph. **Sources** in
`raw/` are read-only; everything in `wiki/` is the LLM's output, edited
freely.
