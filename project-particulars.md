## ★ PROJECT CONTEXT

---

## Project name

AI Governance Wiki

---

## Substantive focus

This wiki is about AI governance writ large. It is intended as a general resource for a set of academic programs concerned with AI safety, regulation, and governance. This will cover technical AI alignment, background on governance and policy in general, AI specific governance both in theory and in practice. It will aim to be authoritative, didactic, and representative of both what is known and what is debated.

---

## The goal this wiki is building toward

This wiki has two principal goals:

1. To stand as a comprehensive resource that represents the collective knowledge of the people who are designing and teaching in these programs.
2. To be queryable in ways that are helpful to building the program — what courses to offer, what those courses should contain, learning outcomes, references, exercises and activities, methods of assessment, and so on.

The wiki will be a comprehensive collection of information, knowledge, and
insights spanning technical safety, governance and regulation, and research strategies and tactics — an aggregation of everything the researcher(s) learns and wants to have available as an archive of its work and raw material for subsequent work.

---

## Audience calibration

All explanatory writing should be pitched to professors who share a focus but who come from different disciplines. They are actively engaged with AI governance but may not be trained in computer science or engineering. They may be philosophers, economists, social scientists, law professors or humanists. Always be comprehensive but do not over-explain. Assume a growing familiarity with AI and ML and make connections to other fields in the arts and sciences whenever that's germane. Precision and density are virtues. Hedging and hand-holding are not.

## Domain tags to use

A flat tag taxonomy (Obsidian convention). Edit freely — these are starting
points, not constraints.

**Substantive domains**
- `technical-safety` — alignment, interpretability, evaluations, scalable oversight, dangerous-capability assessment
- `governance-theory` — regulatory theory, institutional design, soft vs. hard law, capture, legitimacy
- `ai-policy` — AI-specific statutes, executive orders, agency rulemaking, comparative law
- `international` — multilateral fora, comparative regimes, treaty analogues, summits
- `compute` — compute governance, export controls, semiconductor and supply-chain policy
- `industry-practice` — Responsible Scaling Policies, Frontier Safety Frameworks, voluntary commitments

**Risk frames**
- `risk-existential` — catastrophic and large-scale societal risks from advanced systems
- `risk-misuse` — biosecurity, cyber, persuasion and influence, fraud
- `risk-systemic` — labor displacement, market concentration, epistemic ecosystem effects
- `fairness` — bias, discrimination, equity, accountability
- `democratic` — civic functions, information ecosystems, deliberation, elections
- `environmental` — energy, water, supply-chain externalities

**Disciplinary lenses**
- `philosophy` — ethics, philosophy of mind, philosophy of science
- `law` — liability, IP, constitutional questions, administrative law
- `economics` — market structure, antitrust, industrial policy, innovation
- `sts` — science and technology studies, sociology of expertise
- `llm` — LLM-specific technical material

**Cross-cutting**
- `pedagogy` — curriculum design, learning outcomes, case methods, assessment
- `analogy` — comparison with prior regulatory regimes (nuclear, pharmaceutical, aviation, financial, environmental)
- `empirical` / `theoretical` — meta-tags for the kind of claim a page makes
